# cfri-lite methodology

## 1. Purpose

Water-sector cybersecurity tools describe *technical* exposure: missing controls, exposed devices and alerts. Utility finance offices set rates, hold reserves and plan capital in *dollars*. `cfri-lite` connects the two. It turns scenario assumptions into a distribution of annual losses and compares the worst case with the utility's capacity to absorb it.

## 2. Loss model

For each scenario *s* and each simulated year:

- **Occurrence.** A successful incident happens with probability `p_s × Π_c (1 − r_{c,s})`, where `r_{c,s}` is the probability reduction from each selected control *c*.
- **Severity.** The loss of an incident follows a triangular distribution `Tri(low_s, mode_s, high_s)`, multiplied by `Π_c (1 − v_{c,s})`, where `v_{c,s}` is the severity reduction from each control.
- **Annual loss** is the sum over scenarios.

Version 0.1 treats each scenario as at most one material incident per year (Bernoulli) and treats scenarios as independent. Both are simplifications; see the roadmap.

The triangular distribution was chosen because finance staff can supply its three parameters (lowest, most likely and highest plausible loss) without fitting a statistical model, and because Excel can reproduce it exactly.

**Common random numbers.** The same uniform draws are reused when controls are switched on or off. A difference between two control sets therefore reflects the controls, not sampling noise.

## 3. Risk measures

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Expected loss | Mean simulated annual loss |
| VaR95 | 95th percentile of the annual loss |
| **ES95** | Mean annual loss in years at or above VaR95, i.e. the worst 5% of years |
| Loss capacity | Reserves + undrawn liquidity (for example, unused credit lines) |
| Loss-to-capacity ratio *r* | ES95 / loss capacity |

ES95 is used instead of VaR because it accounts for how bad the tail is, not only where it starts.

## 4. The CFRI-lite score

```
score = 100 × (1 − 5^(−r))
```

| *r* (worst case ÷ capacity) | Score |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 |
| 0.25 | 33.1 |
| 0.50 | 55.3 |
| 1.00 | **80.0** |
| 2.00 | 96.0 |

Properties:

- Bounded between 0 and 100, and strictly increasing in the worst-case loss.
- A worst-case loss equal to loss capacity scores exactly 80, the threshold of the highest tier.
- The scale is relative to each utility, so a small system with thin reserves can score higher than a large system with bigger dollar losses.

Tiers: 0–20 Low, 20–40 Guarded, 40–60 Elevated, 60–80 High, 80–100 Severe. The lower tier boundaries are a default and can be changed in `index.py`.

## 5. Ranking and choosing controls

- **Standalone value.** Each control is evaluated alone against no controls. The outputs are expected-loss reduction, ES95 reduction and `ROI = (expected-loss reduction − annual cost) / annual cost`. The report ranks controls by ES95 reduction per dollar.
- **Best set within a budget.** For up to 14 controls the optimizer tries every combination whose annual cost fits the budget. It keeps the one with the lowest ES95, or with the lowest expected loss plus control cost under `--objective total_cost`. For more controls it adds controls greedily by improvement per dollar.

Control effects combine multiplicatively, so two controls that each cut a probability by 50% leave 25% of it, not 0%.

## 6. Synthetic profiles

`data/synthetic/` holds three utilities generated by `cfri_lite/synthetic.py`:

| Profile | Population | Reserves | Undrawn liquidity | Loss scale | Probability scale | Control-cost scale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| small_rural | 8,000 | $0.9M | $0.25M | 0.08× | 1.25× | 0.10× |
| mid_size (reference) | 250,000 | $15M | $5M | 1.0× | 1.0× | 1.0× |
| large_metro | 1,500,000 | $150M | $100M | 5.0× | 1.10× | 4.0× |

All probabilities, loss ranges, control costs and control effects are **illustrative assumptions** chosen to be plausible in order of magnitude. They are not calibrated to incident data. Do not cite them as estimates of real-world losses.

## 7. Input format

```json
{
  "name": "My Utility",
  "reserves": 15000000,
  "undrawn_liquidity": 5000000,
  "synthetic": false,
  "scenarios": [
    {"id": "S1", "name": "Chemical-dosing tampering", "annual_probability": 0.02,
     "loss_low": 500000, "loss_mode": 2000000, "loss_high": 12000000}
  ],
  "controls": [
    {"id": "C5", "name": "PLC setpoint limits", "annual_cost": 80000,
     "probability_reduction": {"S1": 0.2}, "severity_reduction": {"S1": 0.6}}
  ]
}
```

Optional fields: `population_served`, `annual_operating_budget`, `horizon_years`, `notes`, `category` and `description`.

## 8. Limitations

- The model is only as good as its inputs. Probabilities and loss ranges for rare events are uncertain, so use ranges and sensitivity tests.
- Scenarios are independent, and each has at most one incident per year (version 0.1).
- Insurance is modeled as a flat severity reduction. Deductibles, limits and exclusions are not modeled separately.
- Losses are financial. Public-health harm and loss of life cannot be expressed in this score and must be weighed separately.
- This is a planning tool. It is not a forecast, an audit, a regulatory compliance assessment or investment advice.
